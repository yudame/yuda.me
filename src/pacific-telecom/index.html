Practice Area

Pacific telecom practice

Mobile experience, digital financial services integration, and capacity transfer for operators serving the Pacific subregion.

Yudame is building a practice for telecom operators in the Pacific subregion: mobile experience, digital financial services integration, and a handover patient enough that the local engineering team ends up owning everything we ship together.

Why this space

The Pacific subregion is one of the hardest places on earth to run digital services. Connectivity is uneven, devices skew low-end, and bandwidth costs real money. A user might jump from a 4G hotspot to a dying rural cell before breakfast. Software written for first-world latency budgets breaks here, and it breaks silently. What survives is software written for the conditions.

The Asian Development Bank's work on inclusive digital transformation for Asia and the Pacific has narrowed, in recent years, to two concrete levers. First, digital public infrastructure: the identity, payment, and data-exchange rails that let modern services exist at all. Second, digital financial services that close the financial-inclusion gap. By ADB's own count, more than 30% of adults across several Pacific developing member countries sit outside the formal financial system. Operators are where that gap closes. They own the SIM, the device relationship, and increasingly the wallet rails that walk first-time users into formal finance.

Our practice starts inside that reality, not next to it. We build mobile experiences that hold up under intermittent connectivity, and we integrate with the digital financial services stack carriers are extending to underserved users. Then we hand the working knowledge across, so the in-country engineering team owns what we built together. What we're after is capacity in the country that stays there after we go.

What we bring

Mobile UX for real conditions

Product and interface work built for low-end devices, intermittent connectivity, expensive data, and offline-first patterns. Speed and resilience are designed in from the first commit, not sprinkled on at the end.

Digital financial services integration

Hands-on integration with the wallet, KYC, and payments surfaces carriers use to bring first-time users into digital financial services. We build it to interoperate with the wider digital public infrastructure stack as that stack matures.

IP transfer to the client

Code, documentation, and operational knowledge go to the client by default. We write software our partners can run and extend on their own, and we count a clean handover as proof the job was done right.

Capacity building for in-country teams

Pair-programming, design reviews, and architecture mentorship with local engineering teams for the length of the engagement. We aim to leave a team that's sharper than the one we met.

Engagement model

How an engagement runs

Three phases. The only score that counts is whether the in-country team is stronger at the end than at the start.

Phase 01 — Discover

We map the field conditions, the device profiles, and the operator's existing rails before a line of code gets written.

Phase 02 — Build

Product and engineering delivered shoulder to shoulder with the in-country team. They're in the code reviews, the design sessions, and the architecture calls from week one.

Phase 03 — Transfer

Repository, runbooks, and operating knowledge handed across. We've succeeded when the team ships the next version without us in the room.
